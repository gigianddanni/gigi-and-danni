# Hook library

The first two lines decide whether the rest exists. On LinkedIn the feed truncates
around 140–210 characters — everything before "…see more" is the whole pitch.

**Use these as structures, not fill-in-the-blank scripts.** A hook lifted verbatim
reads like a hook. Rewrite it in the user's voice, using their actual specifics.

## Rules that outrank the list

1. **Specific beats clever.** "$4,200 in 11 days" outperforms "a surprising result".
2. **No throat-clearing.** Delete "I've been thinking a lot lately about…". Start
   at the second sentence you wrote.
3. **Front-load the tension.** The unresolved thing must appear in line one.
4. **Never promise what the post doesn't pay off.** Bait costs trust once, then
   the algorithm and the reader both stop.
5. **One idea per post.** If the hook needs an "and", you have two posts.

---

## Pattern interrupt
Open by stating something that violates an unwritten norm of your field, flatly
and without apology. The reader stops because the sentence shouldn't be sayable.

- `<Did the thing everyone says never to do>.`
- `I <broke the standard rule>. It was the right call.`
- `<Role> doesn't need <the thing everyone insists is essential>.`

This is the highest-ceiling category and the easiest to get wrong. The norm you
break has to be one your audience genuinely holds, and the post has to *earn*
it — a pattern interrupt with no argument behind it reads as attention-seeking
and costs trust.

## Contradiction
Set up the reader's assumption, then break it.

- `Everyone tells you to <common advice>. I did the opposite and <result>.`
- `<Impressive thing> isn't the flex. <Unglamorous thing> is.`
- `The best <role> I've worked with was terrible at <expected skill>.`
- `I was wrong about <belief> for <N> years. Here's what changed my mind.`
- `<Thing everyone praises> is quietly costing you <specific cost>.`

## Number
A concrete figure in the first six words.

- `<N> <units> ago I <starting state>. Today <current state>.`
- `£<X> of <thing> taught me one lesson I'd pay double for.`
- `I <did action> <N> times. <Small number> worked. Here's the difference.`
- `<N>% of <group> do <thing>. It's the reason most of them <bad outcome>.`

## Confession
Vulnerability that buys permission for the advice that follows.

- `I almost <quit / shut it down / lost it> last <timeframe>.`
- `Nobody talks about <uncomfortable truth of the role>. I'll go first.`
- `I've never admitted this publicly:`
- `The thing I was most ashamed of turned out to be <the asset>.`

Only use when true, and only when the user has offered the detail. Never invent
a confession — see the SKILL.md rule on fabricating facts.

## Scene
Drop the reader into a moment. Strong for story-led voices.

- `<Time>. <Place>. <Thing happening>.`
- `She said <quote> and I didn't have an answer.`
- `The email arrived at <time>. I read it <N> times.`

## Direct address
Names the reader's exact situation back to them.

- `If you're <specific situation>, this is for you.`
- `You don't have a <assumed problem> problem. You have a <real problem> problem.`
- `Stop <common behaviour>. Start <alternative>. Here's why.`
- `You already know <thing>. So why isn't it working?`

## Teardown
Analysis of a concrete example. Highest credibility-per-word.

- `<Company/person> did <thing>. Here's the part everyone missed.`
- `I rewrote <artifact> and <metric> went from <a> to <b>. The three changes:`
- `Here's my actual <asset>, and what I'd change about it.`

## Question
Weakest category on average — most questions are throat-clearing. Only use when
the question is genuinely uncomfortable to answer.

- `What would you have to believe for <status quo> to make sense?`
- `Would you pay for what you're currently giving away?`

## Listicle
Promise structure. Pays off in scannable form.

- `<N> things I'd tell <past self / beginner> about <topic>.`
- `<N> <items> that <outcome>. Number <k> is the one nobody does.`
- `The <N>-<unit> version of <expensive thing>:`

## Testing a hook

Read line one alone. If a stranger who has never heard of the user would keep
reading, it works. If it only works because they know the author, rewrite it.

Then check it against the voice profile's **Never** list before shipping.

## Every line is a hook

The opener only buys the second line. The second line has to buy the third.
Lara Acosta — Kleo's founder, 350k+ followers — treats every line as if it were
its own hook, and it's the single most useful habit to copy from her.

Practically: after drafting, read the post one line at a time and ask *"would I
keep going?"* at each break. The first line where the answer is no is where
readers stop, and everything after it may as well not exist. Cut or rewrite it —
don't just tighten the opener and hope.

This is also why one-idea-per-post matters. A post that changes subject gives
the reader a natural place to leave.
