# Post templates

Structures, not scripts. Pick one that fits the idea, then write it entirely in
the user's voice. If a template and the voice profile disagree, the voice profile
wins.

Each template lists its **shape** and what makes it fail.

---

## 1. The Reversal
> Hook: contradiction. Best when the user has a genuinely unpopular position.

1. State the advice everyone gives.
2. Say plainly that you did the opposite.
3. The result, with a specific number or outcome.
4. *Why* it worked — the mechanism, not the anecdote.
5. The condition where the common advice is actually right. (This is what
   separates it from a hot take.)

**Fails when:** the contrarian position is fake, or step 5 is missing and it reads
as arrogance.

## 2. The Origin
> Hook: scene or confession.

1. The moment things were worst — concrete, sensory, one detail.
2. What you tried that didn't work.
3. The specific turn.
4. Where you are now, stated flatly. No victory lap.
5. The one transferable lesson.

**Fails when:** the low point is vague, or the ending oversells.

## 3. The Teardown
> Hook: teardown. Highest trust-per-word of any format.

1. Name the artifact (a page, an email, an offer, a launch).
2. Show the before — quote it or describe it exactly.
3. The changes, numbered, with reasoning for each.
4. The after, with the metric.
5. What you'd still change.

**Fails when:** the example is hypothetical. Use real ones or don't use this.

## 4. The Numbered Lesson Set
> Hook: listicle.

1. Hook stating the count and the earned right to the list.
2. N items, each: bold claim line, then 1–2 sentences of substance.
3. A closing item that reframes the whole list.

**Fails when:** items are interchangeable platitudes. Every item needs a specific.

## 5. The Objection Handler
> Hook: direct address. The most commercially useful template.

1. Name the objection in the reader's own words — exactly as they'd say it.
2. Concede the legitimate part of it. Genuinely.
3. Reframe: the thing they're actually solving for.
4. Evidence.
5. The small next step, not the big ask.

**Fails when:** step 2 is fake concession ("I get it, but…").

## 6. The Process Reveal
> Hook: number or direct address.

1. The output people ask about.
2. The unglamorous steps, in order.
3. The step everyone skips.
4. Why skipping it produces the exact failure they're experiencing.

**Fails when:** it's generic enough to apply to anyone. Include the user's real tooling.

## 7. The Definition Fight
> Hook: contradiction.

1. A word everyone uses loosely (e.g. "operator", "system", "brand").
2. What people think it means.
3. What it actually means, defined tightly.
4. Why the sloppy definition costs money.
5. The tight definition applied to one example.

**Fails when:** it stays abstract. Step 5 is mandatory.

## 8. The Receipts Post
> Hook: number.

1. The claim.
2. Three pieces of concrete evidence.
3. The caveat — what this doesn't prove.
4. The offer or the invitation.

**Fails when:** the caveat is missing. It's what makes the receipts believable.

## 9. The Question Nobody Asks
> Hook: question (the good kind).

1. The question, stated cleanly.
2. Why it's uncomfortable.
3. Your honest answer, including the part that doesn't flatter you.
4. What changes if the reader answers it too.

## 10. The Contrast Pair
> Hook: contradiction or direct address. Works well as a carousel.

1. Two side-by-side approaches, named.
2. Same situation run through both.
3. Divergent outcomes.
4. The single variable that caused the divergence.

**Fails when:** one side is a strawman.

---

## Choosing

- Selling something → 5, 8, 3
- Building authority → 3, 6, 7
- Building connection → 2, 9
- Fast to write → 4, 10
- Highest ceiling, hardest to write → 1, 3

## Length

LinkedIn: 900–1,600 characters is the reliable band. Under 400 rarely earns
depth; over 2,000 needs an exceptional hook to survive the truncation.
Instagram caption: 400–800. X: single idea, or a thread built from template 4.

Always end by checking the draft against `studio/voice/voice-profile.md`.
